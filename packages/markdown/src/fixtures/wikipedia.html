Euler's identity

In mathematics, Euler's identity is the equality

eiπ+1=0{\displaystyle e^{i\pi }+1=0}

where e{\displaystyle e} is Euler's number, the base of natural logarithms, and π is pi, the ratio of the circumference of a circle to its diameter.[1]

Euler's identity is named after the Swiss mathematician Leonhard Euler. It is a special case of Euler's formula when evaluated for x equal to pi, and it is often cited as an example of deep mathematical beauty, because it joins the five most basic numbers of arithmetic in one short line.

Explanations[edit]

Fundamentally, Euler's identity asserts that the exponential function, evaluated at i times pi, equals negative one. Multiplying by a complex exponential rotates a point in the plane, and a rotation by pi radians sends one to negative one.
